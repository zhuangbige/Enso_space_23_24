## New Article Logic

The new analysis should make a seasonal argument: 2023/24 did not merely have weak event-mean atmospheric coupling. The oceanic warming and external-basin background evolved ahead of, and partly against, canonical Pacific atmospheric responses. The paper should therefore diagnose timing and variable-by-variable synchrony, not only amplitude.

## Practical Plotting Lessons

- Start every figure from a strong-event comparison so the reader sees that 2023/24 is being judged against relevant analogues, not against a generic climatology.
- Keep event-mean reproduction and seasonal diagnosis visually adjacent. This makes the new work a direct extension of Peng et al. rather than a disconnected analysis.
- Put rainfall/OLR next to wind and pressure. The weak atmospheric response is not only a wind problem; it is also a convection and Walker-circulation organization problem.
- Use July 2023 and January 2024 as mechanism zooms only after the seasonal matrix has shown where the timing mismatch is strongest.
