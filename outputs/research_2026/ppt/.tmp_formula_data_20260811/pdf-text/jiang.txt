                                             1. Introduction
                                             El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) is the dominant mode of interannual climate variability in the tropical
                                             Pacific (Bjerknes, 1969; McPhaden et al., 2006; Neelin et al., 1998; Philander, 1990; Rasmusson & Carpen-
                                             ter, 1982), influencing global atmospheric circulation, precipitation, and temperature patterns (Alexander
                                             et al., 2002; Yeh et al., 2018). In the canonical ENSO theory, equatorial Pacific sea surface temperature anomalies
                                             (SSTA) grow through the Bjerknes feedback, which arises from positive coupling between central‐eastern Pacific
                                             SSTA and central‐western Pacific zonal wind anomalies (Bjerknes, 1969). As a result, SSTA in the central‐
                                             eastern equatorial Pacific, commonly represented by the Niño3.4 index (averaged over 170◦ W–120◦ W,
                                             5◦ S–5◦ N), are widely used to monitor and characterize ENSO evolution (Bamston et al., 1997), leading most
                                             studies to focus on SST variability in this region.
                                             However, SST variability in the far eastern Pacific (FEP), particularly in the coastal equatorial region near
                                             80◦ W–90◦ W, was once considered important for the early development of El Niño events (McPhaden &

               in background conditions that favor central Pacific SST growth (Pan et al., 2024). Despite these advances, the role
               of FEP warming has received comparatively less attention during ENSO development.

               The 2023–2024 El Niño event presents an opportunity to re‐examine the role of FEP SSTA in El Niño devel-
               opment. During this event, substantial warming first emerged in the FEP as early as boreal spring, preceding
               significant warming in the central Pacific (Geng et al., 2024; Peng et al., 2024, 2025; Tan et al., 2024). Although
               warm SSTA later extended westward into the central Pacific, the associated atmospheric response remained
               unusual, with zonal wind anomalies over the central Pacific much weaker than typically observed during the
               development phase (Hong et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2025; Peng et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025). This weakened SST‐
               wind coupling contrasts with the canonical Bjerknes feedback mechanism. Moreover, both the timing and in-
               tensity of the coastal warming during 2023–2024 closely resemble several pre‐1980 El Niño events, raising the
               question of whether FEP warming has again become a crucial driver of ENSO development, as observed in those
               earlier events.
               Here we investigate the role of pronounced FEP warming in the development of the 2023–2024 El Niño and its
               association with weakened SST–wind coupling. Our results show that a substantial portion of FEP SST variability
               is different from recently observed ENSO‐related variability, and this component plays a key role in shaping the
               distinctive atmospheric response during the 2023–2024 event. Further analysis shows that early FEP warming
               modulates tropical Pacific air–sea coupling and thereby influences ENSO development.


               2. Data and Methods
               2.1. Observations
               This study uses monthly mean data sets of SST, wind, precipitation, and ocean temperature from 1960 to 2024.
               The SST data are sourced from the Extended Reconstructed Sea Surface Temperature version 5 (ERSSTv5,
               Huang et al., 2017) data set. The 925 hPa wind fields and precipitation data are obtained from the fifth‐generation
               ECMWF atmospheric reanalysis (ERA5, Hersbach et al., 2020). Ocean temperature data from the Ocean
               Reanalysis System 5 (ORAS5, Zuo et al., 2019) are used to estimate thermocline depth, defined as the depth of the
               20◦ C isotherm (D20). Anomalies are calculated relative to the 1960–2024 climatological mean, and linear trends
               are removed from all data sets to focus on interannual variability. El Niño events are defined based on the Niño3.4
               index, with conditions exceeding +0.5◦ C for at least 5 consecutive overlapping 3‐month seasons. Based on this
               criterion, the El Niño events analyzed in this study include 1963–1964, 1965–1966, 1968–1969, 1969–1970,
               1972–1973, 1976–1977, 1977–1978, 1979–1980, 1982–1983, 1986–1987, 1987–1988, 1991–1992, 1994–1995,
               1997–1998, 2002–2003, 2004–2005, 2006–2007, 2009–2010, 2015–2016, 2018–2019, 2019–2020, and
               2023–2024. The 2014–2015 event is excluded from this study as its evolution deviates from that of canonical El
               Niño, partly due to the influence of the northeastern Pacific warm blob (Tseng et al., 2017), as well as other factors
               such as off‐equatorial SST anomalies in the South Pacific (Zhu et al., 2016) and variations in the vertical tem-
               perature gradient of the atmosphere (Hu et al., 2017).


               2.2. Linear Regression Decomposition

               The canonical ENSO signal is represented by SSTA averaged over the Niño3.4 region, while the FEP region is
               defined as 90◦ W–80◦ W and 5◦ S–5◦ N. To isolate the influence of local FEP SSTA on Pacific‐wide SSTA
               evolution, a linear regression approach is applied to separate out Niño3.4‐related variability from the FEP SSTA
               (Chen & Jin, 2023; Hu et al., 2018):

                                                            T(t) = E · TN34 (t) + TR (t),                                        (1)

               where TN34 denotes the Niño3.4 index, E is the regression coefficient, and TR represents the residual FEP SST
               variability after linearly removing the simultaneous Niño3.4‐related component, and is interpreted here as the
               variability not explained by the canonical ENSO signal.
               Previous studies commonly use SSTA in the Niño1+2 region (90◦ W–80◦ W, 0◦ S–10◦ S) to characterize coastal
               eastern Pacific warming. Sensitivity tests show that the results based on the Niño1+2 region are consistent with

               those obtained using the FEP definition adopted here. In this study, the FEP index is used to provide a more direct
               representation of equatorial SST variability and its role in air–sea coupling processes.

               2.3. Atmospheric General Circulation Model

               To investigate the atmospheric response to different SSTA patterns, this study employs the ECHAM version 4.6
               (ECHAM4.6) atmospheric general circulation model (AGCM) developed by the Max Planck Institute for
               Meteorology (Roeckner et al., 1996). The model is configured with a horizontal spectral resolution of triangular
               truncation at T42, corresponding approximately to 2.8◦ × 2.8◦ in latitude and longitude, and includes 19 vertical
               levels using a hybrid sigma‐pressure coordinate system extending from the surface to 10 hPa.

               The control simulation (CTRL) is forced with monthly climatological SST derived from the ERSSTv5 data set
               averaged over 1991–2020 and integrated for 20 years. In the sensitivity experiments, prescribed SSTA are
               superimposed on the climatological SST from April to July to evaluate the atmospheric response during the El
               Niño development stage. Each sensitivity experiment is conducted as a 20‐member ensemble, with initial con-
               ditions taken from different years of the control simulation, in order to reduce the influence of internal atmo-
               spheric variability. The atmospheric response is diagnosed as the difference between each sensitivity experiment
               and the control simulation.


               3. Results
               3.1. Distinct Development Characteristics of the 2023–2024 El Niño
               In most post‐1980 El Niño events (1982–1983, 1986–1987, 1987–1988, 1991–1992, 1994–1995, 1997–1998,
               2002–2003, 2004–2005, 2006–2007, 2009–2010, 2015–2016, 2018–2019, and 2019–2020), the SSTA typically
               develop first in the central Pacific during boreal spring, accompanied by pronounced westerly anomalies
               extending across the central equatorial Pacific (Figure 1a). These westerly anomalies play a key role in amplifying
               SSTA warming through the Bjerknes feedback, facilitating the eastward expansion of coupled SST and wind
               anomalies. Consistent with this evolution, thermocline depth anomalies also exhibit a west‐to‐east propagation,
               with deepening initiated in the central Pacific and subsequently extending eastward, in phase with the SST and
               wind anomalies (Figure S1a in Supporting Information S1). By early boreal summer, warm SSTA usually extends
               into the eastern Pacific, forming a basin‐wide warming pattern characteristic of canonical El Niño development.
               Owing to strong air–sea coupling during boreal summer, this coupled SST‐wind feedback rapidly accelerates the
               growth of SST anomalies across the tropical Pacific.
               In contrast, the 2023–2024 El Niño differed from this typical evolution (Figure 1b). Rather than initiating in the
               central Pacific, SSTA warming first emerged in the FEP as early as boreal spring. These coastal eastern Pacific
               SSTA intensified rapidly and expanded westward into the central Pacific during summer. In terms of SST
               evolution, this eastern Pacific–initiated evolution closely resembles that of pre‐1980 El Niño events (Figure 1c).
               Consistent with this SST evolution, thermocline depth anomalies (Figure S1b in Supporting Information S1) also
               exhibit a distinct evolution. Although a west‐to‐east propagating thermocline signal from the western Pacific is
               still present, a more prominent feature is the pronounced deepening in the FEP that subsequently extends
               westward. This east‐to‐west thermocline evolution closely matches the development of SST anomalies, indicating
               a coupled progression of subsurface and surface signals originating from the FEP. Notably, despite pronounced
               equatorial SSTA warming during spring and summer of 2023, the atmospheric response was unusually weak
               (contours in Figure 1b). While Niño3.4 SSTA is typically accompanied by central Pacific zonal wind anomalies
               during El Niño events (Figure 1d), the zonal wind anomaly over the Niño4 region during May–July (MJJ) 2023
               remained near zero, even as Niño3.4 SSTA approached 1 standard deviation.
               The spatial structures of SST and atmospheric circulation during MJJ further demonstrate the distinct evolution of
               the 2023–2024 El Niño. The MJJ period is examined as it corresponds to the onset and early growth stage of El
               Niño events and exhibits distinctive features in the 2023–2024 case. During MJJ 2023–2024, the warmest SSTA
               center was located in the FEP near the South American coast (Figure 1f), in contrast to typical post‐1980 El Niño
               events, in which warming lacks a clearly defined center (Figure 1e). This eastern Pacific warming was accom-
               panied by a tongue of cold SSTA in the subtropical northeastern Pacific, resembling the negative phase of the
               North Pacific Meridional Mode (NPMM, Chiang & Vimont, 2004). Consistent with this SST pattern, precipi-
               tation anomalies exhibited a pronounced meridional contrast, with enhanced convection near the equator and

               3.3. FEP‐Dominant Development in the 2023–2024 and Pre‐1980 El Niño Events
               The previous section showed that the spatial pattern of the residual FEP SST variability closely resembles the
               development structure of the 2023–2024 event. Consistent with this, the time series (Figure 2a) shows that the
               residual component (blue line) is substantially larger than the Niño3.4‐related component (red line) during MJJ
               2023. Together, these results indicate that FEP variability plays a dominant role in shaping the evolution of the
               2023–2024 El Niño. This raises the question of whether similar FEP‐dominant development has occurred in other
               El Niño events. To identify such events, we first select years in which FEP SSTA exceeds 1 standard deviation
               during the May–July development stage. We then impose an additional criterion to exclude cases dominated by
               central Pacific variability, requiring that the residual FEP component exceeds the Niño3.4‐related component
               while the latter remains below 1 standard deviation of the total FEP SSTA. Based on these criteria, five FEP‐
               dominant El Niño events were identified during 1960–2024: 1965–1966, 1969–1970, 1972–1973, 1976–1977,
               and 2023–2024. Notably, except for 2023–2024, all identified events occurred prior to 1980, indicating that FEP‐
               dominant development was more prevalent in earlier decades.
               To assess whether the 2023–2024 El Niño shares similarities with other FEP‐dominant events, composites are
               constructed for the remaining selected years (1965–1966, 1969–1970, 1972–1973, and 1976–1977; Figure 1g). In


JIANG ET AL.                                                                                                                           6 of 11
                    Geophysical Research Letters                                                   10.1029/2026GL123227


               these events, the warm SSTA center is consistently located in the FEP region, similar to the 2023–2024 event.
               This FEP‐centered warming is accompanied by subtropical cooling in the northeastern Pacific, extending from
               approximately 30◦ N southward into the central Pacific near 10◦ N. The subtropical cooling is associated with
               suppressed precipitation around 10◦ N, while enhanced convection develops along the equator, forming a
               meridional precipitation dipole. This meridional contrast in convection induces anomalous northeasterlies north
               of the equator (Figure 1g), producing a circulation pattern closely resembling that of May–July 2023 (Figure 1f).
               To further assess the contribution of residual FEP SSTA to tropical Pacific air–sea variability across different El
               Niño types, we reconstruct the fields of SSTA, surface winds, and precipitation based on the multiple regression
               analysis (Equation 2), with results shown in Figure 3. For the 2023–2024 event (Figure 3a) and the pre‐1980 FEP‐
               dominant events (Figure 3b), the air–sea patterns associated with residual FEP SSTA are pronounced, indicating
               that FEP variability plays a dominant role in shaping the observed atmospheric responses. In contrast, for post‐
               1980 El Niño events (Figure 3c), the residual component accounts for only a small fraction of the observed
               circulation anomalies, suggesting a reduced role of FEP variability in these events.
               To verify the atmospheric response induced by the residual FEP SST component, AGCM experiments are
               conducted using residual FEP SSTA from 2023. When only the positive portion of the FEP warming is prescribed
               (Figure 3d), the simulated response closely resembles the reconstructed patterns from observations (Figure 3a),
               including suppressed precipitation north of the equator and enhanced convection along the equator, forming a
               meridional precipitation dipole. This dipole induces off‐equatorial northeasterlies extending into the central
               Pacific, which oppose and weaken the development of central Pacific westerly anomalies. These results
               demonstrate that FEP warming alone is sufficient to drive the observed atmospheric response. We next assess the
               role of the accompanying subtropical cooling by prescribing the full residual FEP SSTA pattern (Figure S5 in
               Supporting Information S1). A similar atmospheric response is obtained, despite with a stronger amplitude. This
               suggests that FEP warming acts as the primary driver of the circulation anomalies, while the associated sub-
               tropical cooling enhances the atmospheric response.

               3.4. Possible Decadal Modulation of ENSO Development Pathways

               The occurrence of FEP‐dominant development in both the pre‐1980 period and 2023–2024 suggests that ENSO
               development pathways may be modulated on decadal timescales. To explore this, we examine decadal variations
               in the lead–lag relationship between Niño3.4 and the residual FEP SSTA, together with changes in the back-
               ground thermocline structure (Figure 4). The lead–lag correlation exhibits pronounced decadal variability
               (Figure 4a). Before the 1980s, residual FEP SSTA tends to lead the Niño3.4 index, suggesting that ENSO
               development preferentially originates in the FEP. From the 1980s to the early 2000s, this relationship reverses,
               with SSTA in the central Pacific tending to precede that in the FEP. In recent decades, residual FEP SSTA again
               leads Niño3.4, consistent with the development characteristics of the 2023–2024 event. These results indicate that
               the dominant region of ENSO development shifts on decadal timescales.
               Previous studies have suggested that the spatial characteristics of ENSO SSTA development may be modulated
               by the background thermocline structure in the equatorial Pacific (Fedorov & Philander, 2000; Yeh et al., 2009;
               Yu & Kao, 2007). To assess whether changes in thermocline structure are linked to the decadal modulation of
               ENSO development pathways, we examine the evolution of the zonal structure of equatorial Pacific thermocline
               depth (Figure 4b). The results show that the thermocline exhibits pronounced decadal variability, and the timing
               of these changes closely aligns with the transition in the lead–lag relationship shown in Figure 4a. During the pre‐
               1980 period, the thermocline exhibits a relatively steep west–east gradient, with a shallower thermocline in the
               FEP. This configuration may increase the sensitivity of FEP SST to local wind forcing and upwelling anomalies,
               favoring ENSO development originating from this region. In contrast, during 1977–2004, the thermocline be-
               comes anomalously shallow over the central Pacific (Figure 4b), while the zonal gradient is reduced. This
               structural change may enhance the sensitivity of central Pacific SST to zonal wind stress forcing, promoting
               ENSO development in the central Pacific. In recent decades (2005–2024), the thermocline structure shifts again,
               with a shallower eastern Pacific and a deeper western Pacific (Figure 4b). This background state increases the
               sensitivity of SST variability in the eastern Pacific during ENSO development, providing favorable conditions for
               FEP‐dominant SST growth.

               This interpretation is further supported by theoretical and observational studies showing that ENSO character-
               istics depend sensitively on the background thermocline structure and mean wind stress, which jointly regulate the

               4. Conclusion and Discussion
               This study revisits the role of FEP SSTA in shaping El Niño development, focusing on the 2023–2024 event.
               Unlike post‐1980 El Niño events, which develop from the central Pacific with strong westerly anomalies, the
               2023–2024 event featured early warming in the FEP region and an unusually weak central Pacific westerly
               response despite comparable SSTA amplitude. This weakening is linked to enhanced off‐equatorial northeasterly
               anomalies that suppress central Pacific westerly development, indicating a distinct development structure. By
               decomposing FEP SSTA into ENSO‐related and residual components, we identify a residual FEP pattern
               characterized by localized eastern Pacific warming, subtropical cooling, a meridional precipitation dipole, and
               enhanced northeasterlies. This pattern closely resembles the observed evolution of the 2023–2024 event, sug-
               gesting a dominant role of FEP variability. AGCM experiments further confirm that residual FEP‐related SSTA
               can drive atmospheric responses consistent with observations. Similar FEP‐dominant characteristics are also
               found in several pre‐1980 events, whereas post‐1980 events exhibit weaker FEP variability and stronger central
               Pacific–dominated air–sea coupling.
               The occurrence of FEP‐dominant development in both the pre‐1980 period and the 2023–2024 event is likely
               linked to the decadal modulation of the background thermocline structure. This is supported by the decadal shifts
               in the lead–lag relationship between Niño3.4 and residual FEP variability, which closely correspond with changes
               in thermocline structure. In particular, variations in thermocline slope and depth structure may modulate the
               relative sensitivity of the eastern and central Pacific to SST growth, thereby influencing where ENSO prefer-
               entially develops. However, further coupled model experiments are needed to clarify the mechanisms linking
               thermocline changes to shifts in ENSO development pathways.
               Recent studies suggest that differences in zonal SSTA patterns during the developing stage can influence the
               eventual type of El Niño events, including eastern Pacific (EP) and central Pacific (CP) types (R. Wang
               et al., 2026). In this study, among the five identified FEP‐dominant El Niño events (1965–1966, 1969–1970,
               1972–1973, 1976–1977, and 2023–2024), all except the 1969–1970 event are classified as EP‐type events. In
               contrast, among non‐FEP‐dominant El Niño events, approximately 75% (12 out of 16) are identified as CP‐type
               events. The Hovmöller diagrams of equatorial SSTA (Figure S6 in Supporting Information S1) further reveal
               distinct evolutionary characteristics between EP and CP types. For EP‐type events (Figure S6a in Supporting
               Information S1), warming typically initiates in the FEP and subsequently propagates westward, whereas CP‐type
               events (Figure S6b in Supporting Information S1) exhibit initial warming centered in the central Pacific. These
               results suggest that the spatial structure of SSTA during the developing stage may provide a precursor for the
               eventual type of El Niño events.
               In addition to the FEP‐driven processes identified in this study, several external factors may have further
               contributed to the wind–SST decoupling observed during 2023–2024. Previous studies suggest that anomalous
               warming associated with the North Atlantic tripole SSTA, as well as extreme SSTA in the extratropical North
               Pacific, can induce equatorial Pacific easterly anomalies (Hong et al., 2024). Similarly, enhanced heating over the
               Indian summer monsoon and Maritime Continent regions, together with warming in the tropical Atlantic and
               Indian Oceans, can generate easterly anomalies over the equatorial Pacific through Gill‐type responses (Zhao
               et al., 2025). Enhanced convection over the western tropical Pacific and the associated meridional pressure
               gradient anomalies have also been shown to suppress surface westerly wind anomalies (Tan et al., 2024). Cold‐
               phase NPMM conditions may further contribute by inhibiting the development of westerly wind bursts (Lin
               et al., 2025). Furthermore, long‐term background SST trends, characterized by relatively stronger warming
               outside the eastern tropical Pacific, may favor easterly anomalies over the central Pacific (Peng et al., 2025).
               These remote forcings likely acted to reinforce the suppression of the canonical westerly response, thereby
               reducing the westerly anomalies during the 2023–2024 El Niño. The relative contributions of the FEP variability
               and remote forcings remain to be quantified in future work.
